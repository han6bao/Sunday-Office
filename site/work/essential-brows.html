Now · One site that shows the work, answers the questions and books

00 · Where she started

The craft was there. The home wasn't.

Aliya does permanent brows in Milton. Her clients drive in from Tacoma, Puyallup, Federal Way and nearby towns, so most of them decide whether to book before they ever meet her.

A booking link can hold appointments. It can't explain the difference between nano and ombre, show her results, or tell someone whether they're a good candidate. So Aliya answered those questions herself, over and over, in her messages.

She didn't need more clients asking questions. She needed a place that answered them.

So I built her a site that does the explaining, with "Book Your Brows" one tap away on every page.

Brief · Rules I set myself

Four rules before designing anything.

Her feed leads: The site should look like her Instagram: black and white, soft neutrals, close crops on the brow.

Answer before they ask: Every question she gets in her messages should have an answer on the site.

Booking is one tap: Whatever page someone is on, the next step is right there.

Phone first: Her clients find her on their phones, so that's where the design starts.

Side by side · What changed

Then and now.

Home: A booking link → A custom website on her own domain
Questions: Answered one message at a time → A brow guide, a candidate quiz and an FAQ
Prices: Inside the booking app → A full menu with touch-up timing, every button linked to booking
Brand: Her logo, and not much around it → Her logo inside a brand kit: palette, type, buttons and voice
Training: Not shown anywhere → Its own section for one-on-one and small-group training

01 · Look

Built from her feed.

Black and white with soft neutrals, close crops on the brow and plenty of space. Nothing loud.

Her Instagram already had a clear style, and her clients already knew it. Moving from her feed to her site should feel like walking into the same studio.

02 · Structure

Which brow is yours?

Three techniques, each matched to how you live, plus a short quiz that tells people if they're a good candidate before they book.

These were the questions Aliya answered most. Putting them on the site means clients arrive already knowing what they want.

03 · Voice

Words that sound like her.

Honest, calm and clear. A few lines from the site: "Wake up with your brows already done." "Answer honestly." "Don't take our word for it. See the brows."

Someone choosing who works on their face wants to feel at ease. Short, plain lines do that better than big promises.

04 · Booking

Every button goes somewhere.

The full menu with prices and touch-up timing, and every button linked straight to her booking system.

People who know the price and the timing are ready to book. The site gets them there in one tap.

05 · Phones

Designed for the phone in her hand.

Every page was designed at phone size first: the brow guide, the quiz, the gallery and the booking buttons.

Her clients find her through Instagram and links from friends, so the first impression almost always happens on a phone.

06 · Setup

Everything plugged in.

I set up her domain and an email for inquiries, and linked every button to her booking system. I walked her through each step with plain instructions.

A site isn't done until it works on its own. She left with everything connected and knew how it all fit together.

A note on credit

Her logo. My system around it.

The Essential Brows logo is Aliya's own mark, and the photos are hers. I didn't make either. What I built is everything around them: the palette, the type, the buttons, the voice and the website.
