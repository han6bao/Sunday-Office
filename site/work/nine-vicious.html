00 · Where they started

A jeweler, an artist and one set of grillz.

Marcus Adam (@maarcusadam) is a private jeweler who makes custom grillz for artists. He wanted his work in front of more of the right people.

Nine Vicious is a rapper from Georgia who was signed to Young Thug's YSL Records. Pitchfork has compared his early work to Young Thug and Ken Carson.

My job was to photograph Nine wearing the green set and build the promotion around those photos.

The piece

Photos of the grillz by Marcus Adam

Brief · Rules I set myself

Four rules before the shoot.

The grillz are the subject: The photos have to show the piece clearly, even when the person wearing it is well known.

The right person wears it: A good photo of the right person wearing the piece shows it better than a product shot.

Shoot for the promotion: The photos had to work as posts, since the promotion would be built around them.

Credit the maker: Marcus made the grillz. The work should lead people back to him.

01 · Subject

The piece, on the artist.

I photographed Nine Vicious wearing the grillz instead of shooting them on their own.

Marcus makes grillz for artists. Seeing them on an artist shows people what they look like worn, and who they are made for.

02 · Focus

One set, the green one.

The whole shoot centered on a single set of grillz.

Keeping to one piece gave the posts one clear thing to look at and talk about.

03 · Promotion

Built around the photos.

After the shoot I handled the marketing and promotion, with my photos of Nine at the center of it.

For a custom jeweler, visibility is how new clients find him. A well-known artist wearing the piece was the strongest thing we had to show.

Results · What happened

His most-liked post, and then some.

It became the most-liked post on Marcus's page. For a small business on Instagram, that matters more than it sounds. When people like, save and share a post, Instagram shows it to more people, including people who don't follow him yet. One strong post can keep putting his work in front of new eyes long after it goes up.

It also gave him something money can't easily buy: a known face wearing his work. When someone new finds his page and sees an artist like Nine Vicious in his grillz, they trust him faster. That kind of credibility helps a custom jeweler turn views into real orders.

Then fans started using my photo of Nine as their profile picture on X and other platforms, which I didn't expect. For a custom jeweler, that kind of visibility helps new clients find him.

Picked up by the culture

A music news page used my photo.

When Our Generation Music, a verified hip-hop news page, ran a story on Nine Vicious, they used my photo of him in the red velvet jacket and green grillz. The post reached 3.9K likes, 208 comments and 97 reposts. When the culture reaches for a photo to tell the story, that's the one doing the work.

A note on credit

His grillz. My photos.

The grillz are Marcus Adam's work, and Nine Vicious is the artist wearing them. The photos of Nine are mine, and so is the promotion built around them. The close-up photos of the grillz were taken by Marcus.

The short version

Have something people should see? Let's get it in front of them.

This works without a celebrity too. An artist, a founder or a small business can get the same result when the photos match what they're building.
