WHEN TO DO WHAT

From a week out to the morning of.

01

A week before

Give yourself time, so nothing is rushed.

  • Plan your outfitsTry everything on. Fitted, simple and plain. Send me a photo if you're not sure.
  • Get a haircut or trimAbout a week before, so it looks like you, not fresh from the chair.
  • Ask your agency what they wantIf an agency asked for something specific, send it to me now so we can plan for it.
  • Drink more waterIt shows in your skin. Start now, not the night before.
  • Skip anything new on your skinNo new products, facials or treatments this week. Stick to what you know works.

02

The day before

Get everything ready, so the morning is easy.

  • Wash and press your clothesSteam or iron them, then hang them up. Wrinkles show on camera.
  • Pack your bagUse the list below and tick things off as you go.
  • Trim and clean your nailsHands show up in more photos than you think. Clean and neutral is best.
  • Confirm the time and placeCheck the address, parking and how long it takes to get there.

03

The night before

Rest is the best thing you can do for your face.

  • Sleep wellAim for a full night. Tired eyes are hard to hide in natural light.
  • Go easy on salt and alcoholBoth can make your face puffy the next morning.
  • MoisturizeYour usual routine, nothing heavy.

04

The morning of

Keep it simple and arrive calm.

  • Clean, moisturized skinLight or no makeup. Nothing that changes your face.
  • Eat somethingYou'll be standing, moving and holding poses for a while.
  • Wear something loose thereSoft clothes and no tight socks, so you don't arrive with marks on your skin.
  • Arrive a few minutes earlyTime to settle in, check your hair and get comfortable before we start.

WHY THE MIRROR

Ellis didn't need one.

LOFT in Tacoma has a big mirror we used for touch-ups between looks. Not every space has one, so a small handheld mirror is worth packing. It saves time and you'll feel more ready in every frame.

WHAT TO AVOID

Keep it simple.

Digitals show who you are, not what you're wearing. Anything that pulls attention away from you works against you.

  • Brands, logos and graphic prints
  • Busy patterns and bright neon
  • Baggy or oversized clothes that hide your shape
  • Heavy makeup, false lashes and contour
  • Big jewelry, hats and sunglasses
  • Fresh tan lines or a new spray tan

HAIR

Wear it like you would to a casting.

Short hair

Clean and natural, the way you'd wear it to a casting. Avoid a lot of shiny product so the shape reads. Bring a comb for flyaways.

Long hair

Wear it down and natural first, then pulled back off the face so agencies can see your face shape and neck. Bring hair ties and bobby pins.

Curls, coils + locs

Show your natural texture as it is today. Bring a spray bottle and your usual product to refresh between looks.

Facial hair

Come the way you'd show up to a job: freshly shaved, or trimmed and neat. If you're not sure which way to go, ask your agency first.

POSING

It's in your face and your energy.

01

Your energy shows

Digitals are simple, so the feeling you bring and what your face says are what come through most strongly. Relaxed and present always reads better than posed.

02

Eyes alive

Think of a person or a moment, not the camera. A thought behind the eyes keeps them from going flat.

03

Breathe out before each frame

It drops your shoulders and softens your jaw.

04

Chin slightly forward

Forward and a touch down, which defines your jawline without looking stiff.

05

Hands relaxed

Loose at your sides or lightly in your pockets. No fists, no stiff fingers.

06

Stand tall, not stiff

Weight on one leg, shoulders down and back. Small shifts between frames give you range.

07

A smile and a serious look

Agencies want both, so we'll get both. A real smile, not a held one.

08

I'll guide you

You don't need to know any of this by heart. I direct every angle and expression the whole time.
