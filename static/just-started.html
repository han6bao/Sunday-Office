A FREE GUIDE · NEW BUSINESSES IN WASHINGTON

I just started a business. Now what?

Everything a new business needs, in the order that saves you time and money. Use it on your own, tick things off as you go, and reach out only if you want help with a step.

STAGE 01

Make it official

Do these first. Everything else depends on them.

  1. Choose your business structure

    Sole proprietor, LLC or corporation. Free advisors can help you decide.

    Free advising in Washington →

  2. Register with the state

    LLCs and corporations file with the Washington Secretary of State.

    Where to file →

  3. Get your business license

    Through the Department of Revenue's Business Licensing Service, plus your city license if you have one.

    License links →

  4. Open a business bank account

    Keep business money separate from day one. It makes taxes much easier.

  5. Get an EIN if you need one

    Free from the IRS. You'll need it to hire or for some bank accounts.

    Where to get it →

STAGE 02

Know who you're for

Ten minutes of thinking that makes every later decision easier.

  1. Describe your ideal customer

    Who they are, what they need and where they already look for it.

  2. Write one sentence about what you do

    If a stranger can repeat it back, it works. Use it everywhere.

  3. Look at five businesses like yours

    Pick ones you admire. Notice what they all share, then find what only you bring.

    How to stand out →

STAGE 03

Build the brand

Do this before the website and the flyers, so everything matches.

  1. A logo that works small and large

    On a sign, a receipt and a profile picture.

  2. Colors, fonts and your voice

    A short guide so everything you make looks like it came from the same place.

    Build a World →

  3. Templates you can use yourself

    Posts and stories in your brand, editable in Canva.

    Social packs →

STAGE 04

Give people somewhere to land

Your home base, so you're not living in DMs.

  1. Buy your domain

    yourname.com, in your own name.

  2. Set up a business email

    hello@yourname.com looks more trustworthy than a personal address.

  3. Launch a simple website

    Home, services, about and a way to book. One good page is a fine start.

    Launching a website →

  4. Connect booking and payments

    One clear button that lets people book or pay without messaging you.

STAGE 05

Look the part

People trust what they can see.

  1. Professional photos of you

    For your site, Google and LinkedIn.

    Headshots →

  2. Photos of your work, space or product

    Real photos beat stock every time.

    Photography →

  3. A few short videos

    Reels that show what it's like to work with you.

    Social + Content →

STAGE 06

Get found

So people who've never heard of you can find you.

  1. Create your Google Business Profile

    Free. Fill in every field and add real photos.

    Showing up on Google →

  2. Use the same name and info everywhere

    Google, Instagram, Yelp and your website should all match.

  3. Ask your first clients for reviews

    Send a link right after a great experience.

  4. Join a local network

    Chambers, business groups and free advising programs bring referrals.

    Washington resources →

STAGE 07

Tell people you're open

A launch you plan beats a launch you hope for.

  1. Plan a launch post and a few weeks after

    Introduce yourself, show the work, say how to book.

  2. Pick two platforms

    Do two well instead of five badly. For most local businesses: Google and Instagram.

  3. Make a simple monthly content plan

    A mix of your work, your people, behind the scenes and offers.

    Social media content →
