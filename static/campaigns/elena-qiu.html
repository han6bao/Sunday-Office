THE DESIGNER

Elena Qiu is a Taiwanese-American designer who grew up in Seattle and now works in New York. She makes custom, one-of-a-kind womenswear with bold silhouettes, statement shoulders and layered textures, so women can take up space freely. Her work has been in Vogue, and she's been interviewed by CNN.

THE LOOK

Avant-garde with a little early 2000s Harajuku in it: asymmetric cuts, straps, ruffles and worn-in whites. It reads almost apocalyptic, like clothes that survived something. The photos needed to feel the same way.

THE THINKING

How it feels, not how it's made.

These are editorials from her 2023 show. I've shot for her for years, and my photos have been part of her social media for a long time. For this show, my job was the feeling, not the details. Here's how I approached it.

  1. 01

    Feeling over details

    Other photographers at the show covered the details and specifics. My job was the story: how the outfits make you feel, and how they move when someone wears them.

  2. 02

    Video and storytelling

    I shot as much film as stills. A few seconds of someone walking toward you says more about a garment than a flat lay ever will.

  3. 03

    Directed posing

    I helped the models find poses that suit the clothes: low, close to the wall, leaning on each other. Nothing stiff, nothing that looks like a catalog.

  4. 04

    Cold, cinematic color

    Washed whites, blue-green shadows and soft contrast, so the set feels like a still from a film instead of a product page.

  5. 05

    Blurry on purpose

    A little motion blur keeps it effortless and real. The clothes look worn and lived in, the way they're meant to be.

  6. 06

    A little haunting

    Darker and quieter than a typical show recap, to match the mood of her work.
