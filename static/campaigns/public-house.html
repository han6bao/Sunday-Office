Photography

Notes by Hana · Sunday Office

A bar that glows. Shot the way it looks.

I photographed Public House, a bar and event space in Pioneer Square, for their social media. One question guided the shoot: would the photos feel like standing in the room at night?

00 · Where they started

A room with character. A feed that needed it.

Public House is a bar and event space in an old Pioneer Square building. The room has dark wood, foil ductwork across the ceiling and a view straight into a white-tiled kitchen.

They needed photos for their social media. I shot the space itself: the lit back bar, the neon, the bottles and the tile.

Brief · Rules I set myself

Four rules before the first frame.

The back bar leads

The lit arch is the first thing you notice in the room, so it comes first in the photos too.

Keep the dark

The rest of the room stays dark in person. The photos should keep it that way.

Shoot it as it is

Warm amber and neon, the way it looks when you walk in.

Vertical only

Everything is made for stories and posts, so nothing needs cropping later.

The calls · Decision by decision

What I decided, and why.

01 · Subject

Start with the back bar.

The call
One lit arch with shelves up to the ceiling. The light sits under the bottles, so the whole wall glows.
Why
It is the center of the room and the part people remember. Building the set around it gives their feed one clear picture of the place.

02 · Light

Keep the room as it feels.

The call
Warm amber under the shelves and neon over the glass, with the rest of the room kept dark. I shot it the way it looks in person.
Why
A bar at night is about the light. Brightening the whole room would make it look like a different place from the one people walk into.

03 · Details

The room around the bar.

The call
Dark wood, foil ductwork across the ceiling, the sign and the view into the white-tiled kitchen.
Why
These details show the old Pioneer Square building the bar lives in. They give the feed more to post than one view of the bar.

04 · Format

Vertical, from the start.

The call
Every photo is 9:16, so it drops straight into stories and posts without cropping.
Why
The photos were made for their social media. Shooting vertical meant every frame was ready to post as it was.

05 · Edit

The grain is on purpose.

The call
I added grain to every photo in the edit. It's a taste choice, and it's meant to be there.
Why
A clean, sharp photo of a dark bar can feel flat, like a phone snapshot. Grain gives the photos a film feel that matches the room at night: warm, a little moody, and lived in.

A note on credit

Their bar. My photos of it.

The room, the bar and the name belong to Public House. The photos are mine, made for their social media.
